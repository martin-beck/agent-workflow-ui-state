# AR-0129: Android authoritative decision reliability and parity qualification

Make the registered Android client a complete, reliable decision endpoint. The
service must publish the authoritative multi-AR batch (including both Markdown
documents and decision-specific anchors), persist accepted Android events back
to the Coordinator/AR bridge, and fail closed on stale revisions, expired
credentials, malformed endpoints, and untrusted TLS. The Android client must
survive offline periods, process death, rotation/backgrounding, retries, and
re-registration without losing or duplicating a decision.

## Scope

- Integrate service batch publication and event persistence with the canonical
  AR/TUI bridge envelopes; no local-only success state.
- Exercise complete multi-decision batches, structured proposals, clarify,
  reject, evidence requests, own proposals, Save+Exit, and incomplete exit.
- Make the Android outbox durable and idempotent across reconnect, process
  death, and credential expiry/re-registration.
- Validate HTTPS endpoint scheme/host/certificate policy and SSH-rendezvous
  bootstrap metadata before registration.
- Add deterministic JVM/protocol tests and an opt-in emulator/integration
  journey; document physical-device smoke evidence separately.

## Acceptance evidence

- A coordinator-shaped batch is published, retrieved by a registered client,
  and every accepted event is persisted in the authoritative service journal
  with session/revision/digest binding.
- Restart/offline/retry tests prove no lost or duplicated events, while stale,
  expired, revoked, or wrong-project submissions are rejected.
- Android tests cover rotation/background/process recreation, multi-decision
  rendering and structured proposal submission; emulator qualification is
  reproducible when the local runner is available.
- Endpoint and TLS validation rejects HTTP, missing host, malformed rendezvous
  fields, and untrusted certificates; no private key or credential is placed
  in QR or logs.
- Product PR, state PR, release qualification, and synchronized compatibility
  evidence all point at the same immutable product commit.
